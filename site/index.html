Kubernetes port-forwards in your menu bar.

Set up a Forward once, then switch it on. Aim it at a Service and Kubeport follows the pods: after a rollout or a crash, the next connection to localhost reaches a Ready pod.

Install with Homebrew
brew tap deki-lab/tap
brew trust deki-lab/tap
brew install --cask deki-lab/tap/kubeport
Download the .dmg Source on GitHub

Free and open source under the MIT license. For macOS 14 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs. Kubeport is not signed with a Developer ID, so the first launch needs one extra step.

A rollout no longer breaks your connection

kubectl port-forward svc/… picks one pod and dies with it. Kubeport watches the Service: when that pod stops being Ready, new connections go to another Ready pod. Connections already open to the old pod close.

Pick from your cluster, then flip a switch

Kubeport lives in the menu bar, in the style of the macOS Wi-Fi menu. No Dock icon, no window to manage.

Guided setup
Pick the Context, namespace, Service or Pod and port from lists read from your cluster. Kubeport fills in the local port and the name.
One switch per Forward
Turn a Forward on or off from its row. The master switch turns every Forward off, then brings the same ones back. Forwards that were on when you quit come back at the next launch.
Turns itself off
On a new install, a Forward with no connection for an hour turns itself off. Change the delay in Settings, or per Forward.

Specs

Kubeconfig
Any kubeconfig, including exec auth plugins for EKS, GKE, AKS and kubelogin. No kubectl needed.
Memory
About 17 MB with five Active Forwards.
Languages
English and French.
Appearance
Light and dark, following macOS.

Install

Kubeport needs macOS 14 or later, on an Apple silicon or Intel Mac.

With Homebrew

Terminal
brew tap deki-lab/tap
brew trust deki-lab/tap
brew install --cask deki-lab/tap/kubeport

brew trust is there because Homebrew 7 and later asks you to trust third-party taps.

Or download the .dmg from the latest release and drag Kubeport to Applications.

First launch

Kubeport is not signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS blocks it the first time.

  1. Open Kubeport once, then close the alert.
  2. Go to System Settings › Privacy & Security.
  3. Click Open Anyway next to Kubeport, then confirm.

Or remove the download mark from the Terminal, before you open Kubeport the first time:

Terminal
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Kubeport.app

Run it only on a Kubeport installed from the releases page or the Homebrew tap.

Only your account can use a forwarded port

Kubeport opens the same kind of tunnel as kubectl port-forward, with the same limits.

Only your account
The local port checks who connects. Connections from another account or from root are closed at once.
Loopback only
Forwards listen on 127.0.0.1, and on ::1 when your Mac has IPv6. An Active Forward keeps its port until you turn it off, even while it reconnects, so no other process can take it.
No leaked secrets
When a kubeconfig auth plugin fails, Kubeport shows its exit status only, never its arguments, environment or output.

As with kubectl port-forward, a web page open in your browser can reach forwarded localhost ports through DNS rebinding. Read the security notes to see how to guard a forwarded HTTP service.
